## Why deleting the partner's `user_data` on join is safe
- **Their consent screen:** it already says their own data is replaced, not combined.
- **Never read:** a member's app never reads `user_data`.
- **Leaving:** starts them fresh anyway.
- **Last one out:** gets the household's data written back.

## Choices made
- **Which plan is the extra one:**
  1. A founding price is kept.
  2. Then a store plan is kept over ours, since we can stop ours.
  3. Then the owner's is kept.
- **No automatic refunds.** Our extra Stripe plan is set to end at the end of its paid period and flagged for support (`household_two_paid`). A refund for a period already paid is support's call.
- **No new support email.**
- **A pending store purchase holds the household for 7 days** from when it began, and nothing ends it sooner (round 4).
- **The app's word ends nothing; time does** (round 3). A cancelled store sheet can't be confirmed with the store (RevenueCat hears of no purchase), so its hold just lapses: 32 minutes from the tap at most. The app keeps one attempt id for 20 minutes, so a retry is the same attempt and opens at once, with at least 12 minutes of hold left for the store's sheet. A tap after that is a new attempt and waits for the old hold to lapse; the person sees the existing "didn't complete" line.
- **"Pending" is still the app's word** (round 3), because the store tells only the app. It is limited: store holds only, set once, and 7 days from when the attempt began.
- **No early release** (round 4). Round 3 matched a store's report by person, store and time, because RevenueCat carries no attempt id of ours; that could still end an unrelated, newer hold, so it is gone, with the app's sync releasing by the server's clock. A purchase that went through needs no release: every check reads the paid row first, so a household that has just paid is told so, and never sold a second plan.
- **The switch is the boundary** (round 4). The order is: run 0030, then 0031, then set `HOUSEHOLD_ENABLED=true`. With the switch on and the migrations not run, every household read fails closed (503), and since every plan read asks whether the other member pays, that is everyone's plan read, not only households'. That is deliberate: a missing table must never read as "no household".
- **A web checkout ends after 31 minutes for everyone** (round 5), not only for a household. A checkout left open longer has to be started again; in exchange, no checkout can outlive the attempt that guards it.
- **The server ends an open web checkout itself before an invite or a join** (round 5): starting a household is taken as leaving that checkout. A store sheet can't be closed from the server, so the person waits for it (32 minutes at most) unless the purchase goes through first. A checkout that already completed is cleared too: it can't charge again, and the two paid plans reconcile as when both paid before joining.
- **Clearing an attempt is not an early release** (round 5). It is matched by the attempt's own id, after the server has seen at Stripe, or on the person's own row, that it can no longer charge; it never ends a household's hold, which still ends only at its fixed time.
- **The coach's consent check fails closed** (round 4, shipped on main first). A consent read that can't be made is a 503 the app retries, never an AI call.
- **The owner id lives on the server, per account.** It is in `plaid_account_owners`, recorded from Plaid's own answers, rather than as a stamp in the shared blob that either member could edit. Every derived record is matched to it through its account id. That covers records whose account had already been removed.

## Known limits
- **Records keyed by a bank transaction's id:** category corrections and deposit answers. The database cannot match them, because bank transactions are never stored. The other member's app removes the ones for transactions it holds; any others stay as orphaned entries that show nothing.
- **An account the server never saw listed** (linked before this change and not refreshed since) is matched by its item stamp, or by a bank only the leaver linked.
- **Purchases back to back** (round 3). A member who starts one purchase after another can keep the other from starting one. Each hold still ends at its fixed time, and the owner can remove a partner, or a partner can leave.
- **Purchases alone now wait too** (round 5). Someone in no household who cancels a store sheet and comes back more than 20 minutes later (a new attempt), or who starts a web checkout while their store sheet is open, is refused until the first attempt ends: 32 minutes from the tap at most. The checkout screen shows its existing line ("Couldn't reach checkout. Try again in a moment.") for that refusal; no new line was asked for.
- **Joining after a store purchase that was cancelled** (round 5) waits up to 32 minutes from the tap, because a cancelled sheet can't be seen from the server.
- **The founding suites' "no placeholder" checks** (`foundingSeatsWords` 3a-3b, `foundingPlans` 6q, `foundingStoreLink` 1c) now allow only the ids the couple-mode words doc lists as still to come (`tests/_pendingWords.cjs`), and 3b now asserts that the production guard refuses the build while one is left. Any other placeholder still fails them. Once the line is approved, the list is empty and they are exactly as before.
- **A declined pending purchase** (round 4). If the store declines a purchase it called pending (Ask to Buy refused, say), the household stays held to the end of its 7 days, the buyer included, because nothing ends a hold early. Support can clear the hold row in the database; remove and leave still work.
- **A member's own sync cursor** (`plaid_item_cursors`). If it can't be read, that member's sync starts again from the beginning: more is fetched, but nothing beyond what they may already see.
- **The app's own membership read** (`src/lib/persistence.js` `readMembership`) still treats a missing table as no household. Without the table there is no household to read or write; the server and RLS decide access.
